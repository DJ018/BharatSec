Why email authentication matters

Without SPF, DKIM and DMARC, anyone can send email that appears to come from your domain. That is how most invoice fraud and CEO impersonation scams start. Since 2024, Gmail and Yahoo also require these records from bulk senders, and mail without them is more likely to land in spam.

  • SPF lists the servers allowed to send mail for your domain.
  • DKIM signs your messages so receivers can verify they weren't changed.
  • DMARC tells receivers what to do when SPF and DKIM fail, and sends you reports.

A safe rollout

  1. Publish SPF and turn on DKIM in your mail provider.
  2. Add DMARC with p=none and a reporting address, and watch the reports for a few weeks.
  3. Move to p=quarantine, then p=reject once legitimate mail passes.

Frequently asked questions

Can DMARC break my email?

Only if you jump straight to p=reject before every sender is covered by SPF or DKIM. Start with p=none and review reports first.

Why is DKIM shown as not found when I have it?

DKIM keys live under a selector name chosen by your provider. We check common selectors; yours may use a custom one.

Do I need these if I only receive email?

Yes. Attackers can still send email pretending to be you. A domain that sends no mail should publish v=spf1 -all and a DMARC p=reject record.
