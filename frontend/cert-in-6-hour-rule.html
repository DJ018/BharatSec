Guide

The CERT-In 6-hour rule, explained.

What Indian organisations must do under CERT-In's April 2022 directions, and a simple plan to be ready.

What the rule says

On 28 April 2022, CERT-In (the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team) issued directions under Section 70B(6) of the Information Technology Act. They came into force in June 2022 and apply to service providers, intermediaries, data centres, companies and government bodies.

The headline requirement: report listed cyber incidents to CERT-In within 6 hours of noticing them or being told about them.

Which incidents count

The directions list incident types including:

  • data breaches and data leaks;
  • ransomware and other malicious code;
  • unauthorised access to IT systems, data or social media accounts;
  • website defacement or malicious code inserted into a website;
  • phishing, identity theft and spoofing;
  • denial-of-service attacks;
  • attacks on servers, cloud systems, IoT devices, payment systems and critical infrastructure;
  • targeted scanning or probing of critical networks.

The other duties

  • Logs: enable logs for all ICT systems and keep them for a rolling 180 days within India.
  • Clocks: synchronise system clocks with NIC or NPL time servers, so logs line up.
  • Point of contact: name a person to deal with CERT-In.
  • Providers: VPN, cloud, VPS and data centre providers must keep subscriber details for 5 years.

Not complying can be punished under Section 70B(7) with imprisonment of up to one year, a fine of up to ₹1 lakh, or both.

How to report

Email incident@cert-in.org.in or use the incident reporting form on cert-in.org.in. The first report can be short: what happened, when you noticed, what is affected and who to contact. Send details as your investigation continues.

A one-page readiness plan

  1. Name your CERT-In point of contact and a backup.
  2. Write down who decides that an incident is reportable, and who sends the report.
  3. Keep a short report template ready.
  4. Turn on logging for servers, cloud accounts and admin consoles, with 180-day retention.
  5. Point system clocks at NIC or NPL time servers.
  6. Practise once: run a 30-minute tabletop exercise.
